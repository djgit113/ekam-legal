Tamil Nadu Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Tamil Nadu has 23 gazetted holidays and 1 restricted holiday. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

Gazetted holidays (23)

DateDayHoliday
1 JanuaryThursdayNew Year's DayMarks the first day of the year on the Gregorian calendar, celebrated globally with festivities and new resolutions.
15 JanuaryThursdayPongalThe Tamil harvest festival, thanking the sun for the crop, named after the rice dish boiled over until it spills.
16 JanuaryFridayThiruvalluvar DayHonours Thiruvalluvar, author of the Thirukkural, kept as part of the Pongal season in Tamil Nadu.
17 JanuarySaturdayUzhavar ThirunalThe farmers' day of the Pongal season in Tamil Nadu, honouring those who grow the crop.
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
1 FebruarySundayThai PoosamThe Tamil festival honouring Murugan, marked by pilgrimage and the carrying of kavadi.
19 MarchThursdayTelugu New Year's DayUgadi, the new year of the Telugu and Kannada calendars.
21 MarchSaturdayRamzan (Idu'l Fitr)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer and family meals.
31 MarchTuesdayMahaveer JayanthiMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
14 AprilTuesdayTamil New Year's Day / Dr. B. R. Ambedkar's BirthdayPuthandu, the Tamil new year, falling with the birth anniversary of B. R. Ambedkar.
1 MayFridayMay DayInternational Workers' Day, marking the labour movement's campaign for the eight-hour working day.
28 MayThursdayBakrid (Idul Azha)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
26 JuneFridayMuharram (Yaom-E-Shahadath)The day of martyrdom, kept in mourning for Imam Husayn at Karbala.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
26 AugustWednesdayMilad-un-NabiMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
4 SeptemberFridayKrishna JayanthiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
14 SeptemberMondayVinayakar ChathurthiMarks the birth of Ganesha, remover of obstacles, whose images are worshipped and then immersed.
2 OctoberFridayGandhi JayanthiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement.
19 OctoberMondayAyutha PoojaThe worship of tools, books and machinery on the ninth day of Navratri, kept across Tamil Nadu.
20 OctoberTuesdayVijaya DasamiThe tenth day of Navratri, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the festival.
8 NovemberSundayDeepavaliThe festival of lights, marking the triumph of light over darkness and Rama's return to Ayodhya.
25 DecemberFridayChristmasCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.
