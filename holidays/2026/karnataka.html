Karnataka Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Karnataka has 25 gazetted holidays and 27 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

See these holidays on a calendar, plan your leave around them and get reminders — with Ekam, a free Android app.

Gazetted holidays (25)

DateDayHoliday
15 JanuaryThursdayUttarayana Punyakala, Makara SankrantiMarks the sun's passage into Capricorn and the start of its northward journey, held to be an auspicious time.
26 JanuaryMondayGanarajyotsava (Republic Day)Marks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force.
15 FebruarySundayMaha ShivaratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
19 MarchThursdayYugadi HabbaThe new year of the Deccan, marked with a preparation combining six tastes to represent the year's mixture of experiences.
21 MarchSaturdayKhutub-e-Ramzan (Id-ul-Fitr)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer and family meals.
31 MarchTuesdayMahaveera JayantiMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
14 AprilTuesdayDr. B. R. Ambedkar JayantiMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India.
20 AprilMondayBasava Jayanti, Akshaya TritiyaMarks the birth of Basavanna, founder of the Lingayat tradition, falling with Akshaya Tritiya.
1 MayFridayKarmika Dinacharane (May Day)International Workers' Day, marking the labour movement's campaign for the eight-hour working day.
28 MayThursdayBakridThe festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
26 JuneFridayMoharam Kade DinaThe last day of the Muharram observance, closing the mourning for Imam Husayn.
15 AugustSaturdaySwatantrya Dinacharane (Independence Day)Marks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947.
26 AugustWednesdayEid-MiladMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
14 SeptemberMondayVarasiddhi Vinayaka VrataThe Karnataka observance of Ganesh Chaturthi, the worship of Ganesha as granter of wishes.
2 OctoberFridayGandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
10 OctoberSaturdayMahalaya AmavasyeThe new-moon day closing the fortnight of ancestral offerings and opening the Durga Puja season.
20 OctoberTuesdayMahanavami, Ayudha PujeThe ninth day of Navratri, on which tools, books and machinery are worshipped.
21 OctoberWednesdayVijayadashamiThe tenth day, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the Navratri festival.
25 OctoberSundayMaharshi Valmiki JayantiMarks the birth of Valmiki, author of the Ramayana.
1 NovemberSundayKannada RajyotsavaMarks the formation of the state of Karnataka in 1956, unifying the Kannada-speaking regions.
8 NovemberSundayNaraka ChaturdashiChhoti Diwali, marking Krishna's defeat of the demon Narakasura on the day before Diwali.
10 NovemberTuesdayBalipadyami, DeepavaliThe Diwali day on which King Bali's annual return is welcomed, and cattle and account books are honoured.
27 NovemberFridayKanakadasa JayantiMarks the birth of Kanakadasa, the Kannada poet-saint who wrote against caste exclusion.
25 DecemberFridayChristmasCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.

Restricted holidays (27)

DateDayHoliday
1 JanuaryThursdayNootana Varsharambha (New Year's Day)The first day of the year on the Gregorian calendar.
27 JanuaryTuesdayMadhva NavamiMarks the day the philosopher Madhvacharya, founder of the Dvaita school, is held to have departed.
4 FebruaryWednesdayShab-e-BaratThe night of records, kept with prayer and remembrance of the dead in the month before Ramadan.
2 MarchMondayHoli HabbaThe festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.
17 MarchTuesdayShab-e-QadarThe Night of Power in the last ten days of Ramadan, held to be the night the Quran was first revealed.
20 MarchFridayJumat-ul-VidaThe last Friday of Ramadan, drawing the largest congregational prayers of the year.
23 MarchMondayDevara Dasimayya JayantiMarks the birth of Devara Dasimayya, the tenth-century Kannada poet-saint of the Veerashaiva tradition.
27 MarchFridaySri Rama NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, celebrated with readings of the Ramayana.
4 AprilSaturdayHoly SaturdayThe day between Good Friday and Easter Sunday, kept by Christians as a day of vigil.
14 AprilTuesdaySauramana YugadiThe solar new year of the Deccan, kept a few weeks after the lunar Yugadi.
21 AprilTuesdaySri Shankaracharya JayantiMarks the birth of Adi Shankaracharya, the philosopher who consolidated Advaita Vedanta.
22 AprilWednesdaySri Ramanujacharya JayantiMarks the birth of Ramanujacharya, the philosopher of Vishishtadvaita who opened temple worship to all castes.
1 MayFridayBuddha PurnimaMarks the birth, enlightenment and passing of Gautama Buddha, all traditionally held to have fallen on the same full-moon day.
21 AugustFridaySri Varamahalakshmi VrataA Friday fast kept by married women in southern India for the family's prosperity and well-being.
26 AugustWednesdayRig Upakarma / TiruvonamThe day Rigvedic Brahmins renew the sacred thread, falling with Tiruvonam, the chief day of Onam.
27 AugustThursdayYajur UpakarmaThe day Yajurvedic Brahmins renew the sacred thread, kept on the Shravana full moon.
28 AugustFridayBrahmasri Narayanaguru Jayanti, Raksha BandhanaMarks the birth of Narayana Guru, the Kerala reformer against caste, falling with Raksha Bandhan.
3 SeptemberThursdayKail Muhurta (Kodagu)The Kodava festival at which weapons are cleaned and honoured once the paddy transplanting is done.
4 SeptemberFridaySri Krishna JanmashtamiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
8 SeptemberTuesdayKanya Mariyamma JayantiThe Karnataka observance of the Nativity of Mary, kept by Catholic communities.
14 SeptemberMondaySwarna Gowri VrataThe Karnataka fast kept by women in honour of Gauri, on the day before Ganesh Chaturthi.
17 SeptemberThursdayVishwakarma JayantiWorship of Vishwakarma, the divine architect, on which tools and machinery are honoured.
25 SeptemberFridaySri Anantapadmanabha VrataThe worship of Vishnu as Ananta Padmanabha on the closing day of the Ganesh festival.
18 OctoberSundayTula Sankramana (Kodagu)The Kodava observance of the sun's entry into Libra, kept with a bathing pilgrimage to Talakaveri.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Nanak JayantiMarks the birth of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, observed with processions and continuous reading of the Guru Granth Sahib.
26 NovemberThursdayHuttari HabbaThe harvest thanksgiving of Kodagu, at which the first sheaf of paddy is cut and carried home.
24 DecemberThursdayChristmas EveThe evening before Christmas Day, marked with midnight services and family celebrations.

Gazetted holidays on a Monday or Friday

These sit next to a weekend — a long weekend for anyone who has the Saturday off.

  • Monday, 26 January — Ganarajyotsava (Republic Day)
  • Friday, 3 April — Good Friday
  • Monday, 20 April — Basava Jayanti, Akshaya Tritiya
  • Friday, 1 May — Karmika Dinacharane (May Day)
  • Friday, 26 June — Moharam Kade Dina
  • Monday, 14 September — Varasiddhi Vinayaka Vrata
  • Friday, 2 October — Gandhi Jayanti
  • Friday, 27 November — Kanakadasa Jayanti
  • Friday, 25 December — Christmas

Source

Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms, reference DPAR 16 HHL 2025, dated 17 November 2025.
