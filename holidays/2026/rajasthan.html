Rajasthan Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Rajasthan has 31 gazetted holidays and 19 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

See these holidays on a calendar, plan your leave around them and get reminders — with Ekam, a free Android app.

Gazetted holidays (31)

DateDayHoliday
25 JanuarySundayDevnarayan JayantiMarks the birth of Devnarayan, the folk deity revered as an incarnation of Vishnu in Rajasthan.
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
15 FebruarySundayMahashivratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
2 MarchMondayHolika DahanThe bonfire on the eve of Holi, commemorating the burning of the demoness Holika and the rescue of the devotee Prahlad.
3 MarchTuesdayDhulandiThe day of colours following Holika Dahan, kept across northern India.
20 MarchFridayChetichandThe Sindhi new year, marking the birth of Jhulelal.
21 MarchSaturdayId-ul-FitrThe Islamic festival marking the end of Ramadan, celebrated with communal prayers, feasts, charity, and family gatherings.
26 MarchThursdayRam NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, celebrated with readings of the Ramayana.
31 MarchTuesdayMahavir Jayanti (Mahavir Janma Kalyanak)Marks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
11 AprilSaturdayMahatma Jyotiba Phule JayantiMarks the birth of Jyotirao Phule, the reformer who opened schools for girls and campaigned against caste.
14 AprilTuesdayDr. Ambedkar JayantiMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India and campaigner against caste discrimination.
19 AprilSundayParshuram JayantiMarks the appearance of Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu.
28 MayThursdayId-ul-ZuhaAlso known as Bakrid, this Islamic festival commemorates Prophet Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by prayers and charity.
17 JuneWednesdayMaharana Pratap JayantiMarks the birth of Maharana Pratap of Mewar, remembered for refusing to submit to Mughal rule.
26 JuneFridayMoharram (Tajiya)The Muharram mourning for Imam Husayn, in which tazia replicas of his tomb are carried in procession.
9 AugustSundayVishwa Adivasi DiwasThe United Nations day recognising the rights and cultures of indigenous peoples.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
26 AugustWednesdayBarawafatThe twelfth of Rabi-ul-Awwal, kept as both the birth and the death anniversary of the Prophet Muhammad.
28 AugustFridayRaksha BandhanSisters tie a protective thread on their brothers' wrists in exchange for a promise of protection.
4 SeptemberFridayShri Krishna JanmashtamiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
21 SeptemberMondayRamdev Jayanti, Teja Dashmi and Khejadli Shaheed DiwasMarks the birth of Baba Ramdev of Runicha and of Veer Tejaji, with the Khejarli martyrs who died in 1730 protecting the khejri trees.
2 OctoberFridayMahatma Gandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
11 OctoberSundayNavratra Sthapana and Maharaja Agrasen JayantiThe installation of the sacred pot opening Navratri, falling with the birth of Maharaja Agrasen.
19 OctoberMondayDurgashtamiThe eighth day of Navratri, the most sacred of the nine nights devoted to the goddess Durga.
20 OctoberTuesdayVijaya DashamiThe tenth day, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the Navratri festival.
8 NovemberSundayDeepavaliThe festival of lights, marking the triumph of light over darkness and Rama's return to Ayodhya.
9 NovemberMondayGovardhan PujaMarks Krishna lifting Mount Govardhan to shelter the villagers from Indra's storm, kept the day after Diwali.
11 NovemberWednesdayBhai DoojSisters mark their brothers' foreheads and pray for their long life, two days after Diwali.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Nanak JayantiMarks the birth of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, observed with processions and continuous reading of the Guru Granth Sahib.
25 DecemberFridayChristmas DayCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.

DateDayHoliday

1 FebruarySundayGuru Ravidas JayantiMarks the birth of Ravidas, the fifteenth-century poet-saint of the Bhakti movement.

20 MarchFridayJumat-ul-VidaThe last Friday of Ramadan, drawing the largest congregational prayers of the year.
14 AprilTuesdaySain JayantiMarks the birth of Sant Sen, the poet-saint revered by the Sen community.
14 AprilTuesdayVaisakhiThe Punjabi spring harvest festival and new year, also marking the founding of the Khalsa in 1699.
1 MayFridayBuddha PurnimaMarks the birth, enlightenment and passing of Gautama Buddha, all traditionally held to have fallen on the same full-moon day.
29 JulyWednesdayGuru PurnimaThe day pupils honour their teachers, kept on the Ashadha full moon and dedicated to the sage Vyasa.
3 SeptemberThursdayThadadiThe Rajasthani observance kept by women in the month of Bhadrapada for family well-being.
14 SeptemberMondayGanesh ChaturthiMarks the birth of Ganesha, remover of obstacles, whose clay images are worshipped and then immersed.
16 SeptemberWednesdaySamvatsariThe closing day of Paryushan, on which Jains ask forgiveness of everyone they may have wronged.
25 SeptemberFridayAnant ChaturdashiThe final day of the Ganesh festival, on which the images are immersed; also a major Jain observance.
20 OctoberTuesdayMahanavamiThe ninth day of Navratri, the last before Vijaya Dashami.
29 OctoberThursdayKarva ChauthA day-long fast kept by married women for their husbands' well-being, broken on sighting the moon.
