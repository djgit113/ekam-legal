Odisha Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Odisha has 29 gazetted holidays and 9 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

Gazetted holidays (29)

DateDayHoliday
14 JanuaryWednesdayMakar SankrantiMarks the sun's passage into Capricorn and the start of longer days, celebrated with kite flying, bonfires and sesame sweets.

26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
3 MarchTuesdayDola PurnimaThe full-moon day on which Jagannath is carried in a swing procession, the Odia form of the spring colour festival.
4 MarchWednesdayHoliThe festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.

14 AprilTuesdayMahabishuba Sankranti / Dr. B. R. Ambedkar JayantiThe Odia solar new year, falling with the birth anniversary of B. R. Ambedkar.
1 MayFridayBuddha Purnima / Birthday of Pandit Raghunath MurmuBuddha Purnima, with the birth of Raghunath Murmu, creator of the Ol Chiki script for Santali.
16 MaySaturdaySabitri AmabasyaThe Odia fast kept by married women for their husbands' long life, on the new moon of Jyeshtha.
27 MayWednesdayId-ul-ZuhaAlso known as Bakrid, this Islamic festival commemorates Prophet Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by prayers and charity.
15 JuneMondayRaja SankrantiThe Odia three-day festival marking the earth's menstruation and the first rains, when women rest and swing.
26 JuneFridayMuharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day, Ashura, is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
16 JulyThursdayRatha YatraThe chariot festival of Jagannath, in which the deities are drawn through the streets in great wooden chariots.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
26 AugustWednesdayBirthday of Prophet MohammadMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
27 AugustThursdayJhulana PurnimaThe full-moon day closing Jhulan Yatra, on which Radha and Krishna are placed on a decorated swing.
4 SeptemberFridayJanmastamiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
14 SeptemberMondayGanesh PujaThe worship of Ganesha, remover of obstacles, on the fourth day of the bright fortnight of Bhadra.
15 SeptemberTuesdayNuakhaiThe first eating of the new rice, a harvest thanksgiving of western Odisha and Chhattisgarh.
16 SeptemberWednesdayDay following NuakhaiThe second day of the Nuakhai harvest thanksgiving, kept for visiting family and community feasting.
2 OctoberFridayGandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
17 OctoberSaturdayMaha SaptamiThe seventh day of Durga Puja, marked by the Nabapatrika bathing ritual at dawn.
19 OctoberMondayMaha NabamiThe ninth day of Durga Puja, on which the final major offerings are made.
20 OctoberTuesdayVijaya DasamiThe tenth day of Navratri, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the festival.
24 NovemberTuesdayRasa PurnimaThe Kartik full moon on which Krishna's rasa dance with the gopis is commemorated.
25 DecemberFridayChristmas DayCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.
