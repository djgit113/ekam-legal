Maharashtra Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Maharashtra has 25 gazetted holidays and 1 restricted holiday. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

See these holidays on a calendar, plan your leave around them and get reminders — with Ekam, a free Android app.

Gazetted holidays (25)

DateDayHoliday
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
15 FebruarySundayMahashivratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
19 FebruaryThursdayChhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj JayantiMarks the birth of Shivaji, founder of the Maratha empire.
3 MarchTuesdayHoli (Second Day)The day of colours following the Holika bonfire.
19 MarchThursdayGudhi PadwaThe Marathi new year, on which a decorated gudhi is raised outside the house for prosperity.
21 MarchSaturdayRamzan-Id (Id-Ul-Fitr)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer and family meals.
26 MarchThursdayRam NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, celebrated with readings of the Ramayana.
31 MarchTuesdayMahavir JanmakalyanakMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
14 AprilTuesdayDr. Babasaheb Ambedkar JayantiMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India.
1 MayFridayBuddha PournimaMarks the birth, enlightenment and passing of Gautama Buddha, all held to have fallen on the same full-moon day.
1 MayFridayMaharashtra DinMarks the formation of the state of Maharashtra in 1960.
28 MayThursdayBakri Id (Id-Uz-Zuha)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
26 JuneFridayMoharumThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
15 AugustSaturdayParsi New Year (Shahenshahi)Navroz, the Parsi new year in the Shahenshahi calendar, marking renewal and the start of the Zoroastrian year.
26 AugustWednesdayId-E-MiladMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
14 SeptemberMondayGanesh ChaturthiMarks the birth of Ganesha, remover of obstacles, whose clay images are worshipped and then immersed.
2 OctoberFridayMahatma Gandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
20 OctoberTuesdayDasaraVijaya Dashami, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the Navratri festival.
8 NovemberSundayDiwali Amavasya (Laxmi Pujan)The new-moon night of Diwali, on which Lakshmi is worshipped for prosperity in the year ahead.
10 NovemberTuesdayDiwali (Bali Pratipada)The Diwali day on which King Bali's annual return is welcomed, and account books are opened for the new year.
11 NovemberWednesdayBhaubeejSisters mark their brothers' foreheads and pray for their long life, two days after Diwali.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Nanak JayantiMarks the birth of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, observed with processions and continuous reading of the Guru Granth Sahib.
25 DecemberFridayChristmasCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.
