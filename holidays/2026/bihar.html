Bihar Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Bihar has 30 gazetted holidays and 23 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

Gazetted holidays (30)

DateDayHoliday
23 JanuaryFridayVasant PanchamiMarks the arrival of spring and the worship of Saraswati, goddess of learning and the arts.
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
1 FebruarySundaySant Ravidas JayantiMarks the birth of Ravidas, the fifteenth-century poet-saint whose verses are included in the Guru Granth Sahib.
4 FebruaryWednesdayShab-e-BaratThe night of records, kept with prayer and remembrance of the dead in the month before Ramadan.
15 FebruarySundayMahashivratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
3–4 MarchTuesday – WednesdayHoliThe festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.
21 MarchSaturdayId-Ul-Fitr (Eid)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer, charity and family meals.
22 MarchSundayBihar DiwasMarks the creation of Bihar as a separate province in 1912, celebrated across the state with cultural programmes.
26 MarchThursdaySamrat Ashok AshtamiMarks the birth anniversary of Emperor Ashoka, observed in Bihar as a state holiday.
27 MarchFridayRam NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, celebrated with readings of the Ramayana.
31 MarchTuesdayMahavir JayantiMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
14 AprilTuesdayBhimrao Ambedkar JayantiMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India and campaigner against caste discrimination.
23 AprilThursdayVeer Kunwar Singh JayantiHonours Kunwar Singh, the Bihar leader who fought the British in the uprising of 1857 in his eightieth year.
25 AprilSaturdayJanaki NavamiMarks the birth of Sita, also called Janaki, the daughter of King Janaka of Mithila.
1 MayFridayMay Day & Buddha PurnimaInternational Workers' Day falls on the same date as Buddha Purnima, which marks the birth, enlightenment and death of Gautama Buddha.
28 MayThursdayId-Ul-Zuha (Bakrid)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
26 JuneFridayMuharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day, Ashura, is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
29 JuneMondayKabir JayantiMarks the birth of Kabir, the mystic poet whose verses reject both ritualism and sectarian division.
4 AugustTuesdayChehallumThe fortieth day after Ashura, closing the mourning period for Imam Husayn.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
26 AugustWednesdayBirthday of Hazrat Mohammad SahabMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
4 SeptemberFridayShri Krishna JanmashtamiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
2 OctoberFridayMahatma Gandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
17–18 OctoberSaturday – SundayDurga Puja (Saptami, Mahaashtami)The seventh and eighth days of Durga Puja, the festival's most sacred period.
19–20 OctoberMonday – TuesdayDurga Puja (Mahanavami, Vijayadashami)The ninth and tenth days of Durga Puja, closing with the immersion of the idols.
8 NovemberSundayDeepavaliThe festival of lights, marking the triumph of light over darkness and Rama's return to Ayodhya.
10 NovemberTuesdayChitragupta Puja / Bhai DoojWorship of Chitragupta, keeper of the record of deeds, alongside the Bhai Dooj bond between sisters and brothers.
15–16 NovemberSunday – MondayChhath PujaFour days of offerings to the setting and rising sun, the principal festival of Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh.
25 DecemberFridayChristmas DayCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.

Restricted holidays (23)

DateDayHoliday
1 JanuaryThursdayNew YearMarks the first day of the year on the Gregorian calendar, celebrated globally with festivities and new resolutions.
14 JanuaryWednesdayMakar SankrantiMarks the sun's passage into Capricorn and the start of longer days, celebrated with kite flying, bonfires and sesame sweets.
24 JanuarySaturdayKarpoori Thakur JayantiMarks the birth of Karpoori Thakur, twice Chief Minister of Bihar and an advocate for the backward classes.
2 MarchMondayHolika DahanThe bonfire on the eve of Holi, commemorating the burning of the demoness Holika and the rescue of the devotee Prahlad.
13 MarchFridayRamzan ka Antim JummaThe last Friday of Ramadan, drawing the largest congregational prayers of the year.
22 MarchSundayId-Ul-Fitr (Eid)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer, charity and family meals.
1 AprilWednesdayYearly Closing of Bank AccountsThe annual closing of bank accounts on 1 April, applying to government offices with banking functions.
29 MayFridayId-Ul-Zuha (Bakrid)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
18 JuneThursdayAnugrah Narayan Sinha JayantiMarks the birth of Anugrah Narayan Sinha, freedom fighter and the first Deputy Chief Minister of Bihar.
27 JuneSaturdayMuharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day, Ashura, is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
24 AugustMondayAntim Shravani SomvarThe last Monday of Shravan, the month's most auspicious day for the worship of Shiva.
28 AugustFridayRaksha BandhanSisters tie a protective thread on their brothers' wrists in exchange for a promise of protection.
14 SeptemberMondayHaritalika Teej VratA waterless fast kept by women in honour of Parvati's union with Shiva.
17 SeptemberThursdayVishwakarma PujaWorship of Vishwakarma, the divine architect, on which tools and machinery are honoured in workshops and factories.
25 SeptemberFridayAnant ChaturdashiThe final day of the Ganesh festival, on which the images are immersed; also a major Jain observance.
4 OctoberSundayJivitputrika VratJitiya, a day-long waterless fast kept by mothers for the long life of their children.
11 OctoberSundayDurga Puja Kalash SthapanThe installation of the sacred pot that opens the nine nights of Navratri and the Durga Puja observance.
11 OctoberSundayJayaprakash Narayan JayantiMarks the birth of Jayaprakash Narayan, the socialist leader whose Total Revolution movement challenged the Emergency.
21 OctoberWednesdayDurga Puja (Ekadashi)The eleventh day, following the immersion, kept as a closing holiday in Bihar.
21 OctoberWednesdayShri Krishna Singh JayantiMarks the birth of Sri Krishna Sinha, the first Chief Minister of Bihar and a leader of the freedom movement.
14 NovemberSaturdayChhath Puja (Kharna)The second day of Chhath, on which the fast is broken after sunset with offerings of kheer.
3 DecemberThursdayDr. Rajendra Prasad JayantiMarks the birth of Rajendra Prasad, the first President of India and a leader of the freedom movement in Bihar.
24 DecemberThursdayChristmas EveThe evening before Christmas Day, marked with midnight services and family celebrations.
