Uttar Pradesh Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Uttar Pradesh has 24 gazetted holidays and 33 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

See these holidays on a calendar, plan your leave around them and get reminders — with Ekam, a free Android app.

Gazetted holidays (24)

DateDayHoliday
3 JanuarySaturdayHazrat Ali's BirthdayMarks the birth of Ali ibn Abi Talib, cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad.
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
15 FebruarySundayMahashivratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
2 MarchMondayHolika DahanThe bonfire on the eve of Holi, commemorating the burning of the demoness Holika and the rescue of the devotee Prahlad.
4 MarchWednesdayHoliThe festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.
21 MarchSaturdayId-ul-FitrThe Islamic festival marking the end of Ramadan, celebrated with communal prayers, feasts, charity, and family gatherings.
26 MarchThursdayRam NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, the seventh avatar of Vishnu, celebrated with readings of the Ramayana.
31 MarchTuesdayMahavir JayantiMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
14 AprilTuesdayDr. Bhimrao Ambedkar's BirthdayMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India.
1 MayFridayBuddha PurnimaMarks the birth, enlightenment and passing of Gautama Buddha, all traditionally held to have fallen on the same full-moon day.
27 MayWednesdayId-uz-Zuha (Bakrid)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
26 JuneFridayMoharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
26 AugustWednesdayId-e-Milad / BarawafatThe twelfth of Rabi-ul-Awwal, kept as both the birth and the death anniversary of the Prophet Muhammad.
28 AugustFridayRaksha BandhanSisters tie a protective thread on their brothers' wrists in exchange for a promise of protection.
4 SeptemberFridayJanmashtamiMarks the birth of Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, kept with midnight worship and fasting.
2 OctoberFridayMahatma Gandhi JayantiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
20 OctoberTuesdayDussehra (Mahanavami / Vijayadashami)The ninth and tenth days of Navratri, closing the festival with the victory of good over evil.
8 NovemberSundayDeepavaliThe festival of lights, marking the triumph of light over darkness and Rama's return to Ayodhya.
9 NovemberMondayGovardhan PujaMarks Krishna lifting Mount Govardhan to shelter the villagers from Indra's storm, kept the day after Diwali.
11 NovemberWednesdayBhaiya Dooj / Chitragupta JayantiThe Bhai Dooj bond between sisters and brothers, kept alongside the worship of Chitragupta, keeper of the record of deeds.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Nanak Jayanti / Kartik PurnimaThe birth of Guru Nanak, falling on Kartik Purnima, the full moon on which lamps are floated on rivers.
25 DecemberFridayChristmas DayCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.

19 AprilSundayParshuram JayantiMarks the appearance of Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu.
9 MaySaturdayLok Nayak Maharana Pratap JayantiMarks the birth of Maharana Pratap of Mewar, remembered for refusing to submit to Mughal rule.
28 MayThursdayId-uz-Zuha (Bakrid)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat.
25 JuneThursdayMoharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
4 AugustTuesdayChehallumThe fortieth day after Ashura, closing the mourning period for Imam Husayn.
17 SeptemberThursdayVishwakarma PujaWorship of Vishwakarma, the divine architect, on which tools and machinery are honoured in workshops and factories.
25 SeptemberFridayAnant ChaturdashiThe final day of the Ganesh festival, on which the images are immersed; also a major Jain observance.
11 OctoberSundayMaharaja Agrasen JayantiMarks the birth of Maharaja Agrasen, the legendary king held to have founded Agroha and the Agrawal community.
19 OctoberMondayDussehra (Mahaashtami)The eighth day of Navratri, among the most sacred of the nine nights.
26 OctoberMondayMaharishi Valmiki JayantiMarks the birth of Valmiki, author of the Ramayana.
31 OctoberSaturdaySardar Vallabhbhai Patel and Acharya Narendra Dev JayantiMarks the birth of Vallabhbhai Patel, who integrated the princely states, with that of the socialist thinker Narendra Dev.
8 NovemberSundayNarak ChaturdashiChhoti Diwali, marking Krishna's defeat of the demon Narakasura on the day before Diwali.
15 NovemberSundayChhath Puja ParvFour days of offerings to the setting and rising sun, kept with fasting and river-bank worship.
16 NovemberMondayVirangana Uda Devi Shaheed DiwasHonours Uda Devi, the Dalit soldier of Awadh who fought the British at Sikandar Bagh in 1857.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Tegh Bahadur Shaheed DiwasMarks the execution of the ninth Sikh Guru in 1675 for defending the right to religious freedom.
16 DecemberWednesdayUrs of Hazrat Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, AjmerThe death anniversary of the Sufi saint of Ajmer, drawing pilgrims of every faith to his shrine.
23 DecemberWednesdayChaudhary Charan Singh's BirthdayMarks the birth of Charan Singh, Prime Minister of India and champion of the farming classes; kept as Kisan Diwas.
24 DecemberThursdayChristmas EveThe evening before Christmas Day, marked with midnight services and family celebrations.
