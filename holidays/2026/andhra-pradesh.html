Andhra Pradesh Holiday List 2026

Gazetted and restricted holidays, with dates and weekdays

In 2026, Andhra Pradesh has 24 gazetted holidays and 21 restricted holidays. Gazetted holidays are days when state government offices are closed. Restricted holidays are optional days that employees can apply to take off.

Gazetted holidays (24)

DateDayHoliday
14 JanuaryWednesdayBhogiThe first day of Pongal, on which old possessions are burnt to mark a fresh start.
15 JanuaryThursdayMakara SankrantiMarks the sun's passage into Capricorn and the start of longer days, celebrated with kite flying, bonfires and sesame sweets.
16 JanuaryFridayKanumaThe third day of Pongal, on which cattle are honoured for their part in the harvest.
26 JanuaryMondayRepublic DayMarks the day in 1950 the Constitution of India came into force, replacing the Government of India Act as the country's governing document.
15 FebruarySundayMaha SivaratriThe great night of Shiva, kept with fasting and night-long worship.
3 MarchTuesdayHoliThe festival of colours, marking the arrival of spring and the triumph of good over evil.
19 MarchThursdayUgadiThe new year of the Deccan, marked with a preparation combining six tastes to represent the year's mixture of experiences.
20 MarchFridayEid-Ul-Fitr (Ramzan)Marks the end of Ramadan's month of fasting, celebrated with congregational prayer, charity and family meals.
27 MarchFridaySrirama NavamiMarks the birth of Rama, celebrated in Andhra Pradesh with the ritual marriage of Rama and Sita.
3 AprilFridayGood FridayCommemorates the crucifixion of Jesus Christ, observed by Christians with fasting, prayer and church services.
5 AprilSundayBabu Jagjivan Ram's BirthdayMarks the birth of Jagjivan Ram, freedom fighter, Deputy Prime Minister and campaigner for the depressed classes.
14 AprilTuesdayDr. B. R. Ambedkar's BirthdayMarks the birth of B. R. Ambedkar, principal architect of the Constitution of India and campaigner against caste discrimination.
27 MayWednesdayEid-ul-Adha (Bakrid)The festival of sacrifice, commemorating Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son, marked by the sharing of meat with the needy.
25 JuneThursdayMoharrumThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
15 AugustSaturdayIndependence DayMarks India's independence from British rule on 15 August 1947, observed with flag hoisting and national ceremonies.
21 AugustFridayVara Lakshmi VrathamA Friday fast kept by married women in southern India for the family's prosperity and well-being.
25 AugustTuesdayMilad-un-NabiMarks the birth of the Prophet Muhammad, observed with processions, readings and charity.
4 SeptemberFridaySri KrishnaashtamiMarks the birth of Krishna, kept in Andhra Pradesh with fasting and midnight worship.
14 SeptemberMondayVinayaka ChavithiThe Andhra observance of Ganesha's birth, with clay images worshipped at home and in pandals.
2 OctoberFridayMahatma Gandhi JayanthiBirth anniversary of Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, leader of the Indian independence movement and advocate of non-violent resistance.
18 OctoberSundayDurgashtamiThe eighth day of Navratri, the most sacred of the nine nights devoted to the goddess Durga.
20 OctoberTuesdayVijaya DasamiThe tenth day of Navratri, marking the victory of good over evil and the close of the festival.
8 NovemberSundayDeepavaliThe festival of lights, marking the triumph of light over darkness and Rama's return to Ayodhya.
25 DecemberFridayChristmasCelebrates the birth of Jesus Christ, marked with church services, family gatherings and gift-giving.

Restricted holidays (21)

DateDayHoliday
1 JanuaryThursdayNew Year's DayMarks the first day of the year on the Gregorian calendar, celebrated globally with festivities and new resolutions.
3 JanuarySaturdayBirthday of Hazrath Ali (R.A.)Marks the birth of Ali ibn Abi Talib, cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad.
16 JanuaryFridayShab-e-MerajMarks the Prophet Muhammad's night journey and ascension through the heavens.
3 FebruaryTuesdayShab-e-BarathThe night of records, kept with prayer and remembrance of the dead in the month before Ramadan.

13 MarchFridayJamatul VedaThe last Friday of Ramadan, drawing the largest congregational prayers of the year.
15 MarchSundayShab-e-QadarThe Night of Power in the last ten days of Ramadan, held to be the night the Quran was first revealed.
31 MarchTuesdayMahaveer JayanthiMarks the birth of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth and last Tirthankara of Jainism.
20 AprilMondayBasava JayanthiMarks the birth of Basavanna, the twelfth-century reformer and founder of the Lingayat tradition.
1 MayFridayBuddha PurnimaMarks the birth, enlightenment and passing of Gautama Buddha, all traditionally held to have fallen on the same full-moon day.
3 JuneWednesdayEid-e-GadeerMarks the Prophet Muhammad's declaration at Ghadir Khumm, observed chiefly by Shia Muslims.
16 JuneTuesdayMoharramThe first month of the Islamic year; the tenth day is kept in mourning for the martyrdom of Imam Husayn at Karbala.
16 JulyThursdayRatha YatraThe chariot festival of Jagannath, in which the deities are drawn through the streets in great wooden chariots.
4 AugustTuesdayArbayeen (Chahallum)The fortieth day after Ashura, closing the mourning period for Imam Husayn.
15 AugustSaturdayParsi New Year's DayNavroz, the Parsi new year, marking renewal and the start of the Zoroastrian year.
22 SeptemberTuesdayYaz Dahum ShareefThe eleventh-day observance honouring Abdul Qadir Jilani, founder of the Qadiriyya order.
10 OctoberSaturdayMahalaya AmavasyaThe new-moon day closing the fortnight of ancestral offerings and opening the Durga Puja season.
27 OctoberTuesdayHazrath Syed Mohammed Juvanpuri Mehdi's BirthdayMarks the birth of Syed Muhammad Jaunpuri, revered as the Mahdi by the Mahdavia community.
24 NovemberTuesdayGuru Nanak JayanthiMarks the birth of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, observed with processions and scripture readings.
24 DecemberThursdayChristmas EveThe evening before Christmas Day, marked with midnight services and family celebrations.
26 DecemberSaturdayBoxing DayThe day after Christmas, traditionally when gifts were given to those in service; kept as a public holiday in several countries.
